## Differences from Schmeh's transcript

1. **Order.** Schmeh reads rows across all three blocks (row 1 of block 1, row 1 of block 2, row 1 of block 3, then the second rows, etc.). The photograph shows three separate blocks bounded by lines, so I read them block by block.
2. **Line 02:** `wasftzy` → `was[i|f]tzy`
3. **Line 07:** `xfrfewsw…` → `xfr[i|f]ewsw…`
4. **Line 18:** `raserfdcxxfgdfuam` → `raserfdcxx[i|f]gdfuam`
5. **Line 34:** `hgfnuzzaa` → `hgf[m|n]uzzaa` (the shape fits m better)

In points 2–4 the uncertain glyph has the same form: a vertical stroke with a flat serif at the top. It lacks the top-right hook that every other `f` in the text has (e.g. `poutf` and `raserf` in the same lines), and no dot is visible either, which the other `i`s have. I therefore cautiously prefer `i`. The QWERTZ keyboard context would favour `f` (r-f-e, x-…-f-g), so only the original or a better scan can settle it.

## Notes on the document

- Monospaced typescript; a German typewriter is likely (Z on the top row, Y bottom left).
- The vertical and horizontal lines in the background appear to be the paper's grid or ruling and fold marks, not corrections.
- The blocks are bounded above and below by dashed rules; above them are cropped remnants of further text (probably headings or addressees).
- The handwritten X at line 32 and the blot in line 26 are the only interventions outside the typescript.
